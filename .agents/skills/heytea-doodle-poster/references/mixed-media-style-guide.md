# Mixed-media Poster Guide

Use this reference when the requested poster direction includes crayon, collage,
pencil, dry media, or visibly handmade paper texture.

## Core treatment

- Keep the photographed product as the factual anchor; do not redraw the whole photo.
- Use warm white or white paper with generous breathing room.
- Prefer one rough black line family plus one restrained dry-media color family.
- Let marks meander, vary in pressure, and end imperfectly; avoid uniform vector strokes.
- Use paper tooth, dry breaks, uneven edges, and small overlaps without turning the frame into clutter.
- Keep one clear action between the primitive figure and the object.

## Text poster

Build the poster base first. Construct the crooked title separately, then place it
as a black title layer. The lettering should feel hand-drawn and off-grid, not
like a clean font with a rough filter applied.

## No-text poster

Remove all words, letters, numbers, labels, signatures, and pseudo-glyphs. Let the
micro-worker's pose, tool, contact point, and motion line explain the action.

## Guardrails

- Do not copy a supplied reference's exact silhouette, face, lettering, or layout.
- Do not add official brand assets or imply HEYTEA affiliation.
- Do not let dry-media decoration obscure the photographic object.
- Keep the palette restrained and let one accent carry the visual emphasis.
